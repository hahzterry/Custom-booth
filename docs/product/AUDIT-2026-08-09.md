# LUMEE BOOTH Photobooth — Product & UX Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Mode:** White-box (live product + repository)
**Scope:** `https://diy.lumeebooth.com` (`/` and `/business`) + `/Users/lizzie/photobooth` at `main` `e72e65f`, including the planned move to `mybishbash.app/photobooth`
**Method:** Full source review (38 source files, ~17,986 lines; `app.js` 1,790 / `covers.js` 1,540 / `worker/src/billing.ts` 1,097 / `product.js` 802 read in full), both test suites executed, and hands-on sessions in the live product at 1280×800 and 375×812 as a first-time anonymous visitor — including attempting to buy every paid tier, attempting to contact the business, and completing the Personal customisation flow.
**Commercial scope:** Pricing and packaging are judged on the merits, not only on implementation fidelity (§14).

**Confidence note.** Two limits, stated up front:

- **The camera flow was never exercised.** The audit browser cannot be granted camera access, so the three-shot capture, the strip/cover/Polaroid render path and the iOS Share fallbacks are assessed **from source only**. No finding below claims the capture engine is broken. Items needing a real device are tagged **[verify by hand]**.
- **The Worker is not deployed.** `/v1/*` returns 404 on the live origin, so the entire billing, entitlement and Business API layer is audited **as-coded**, not as-running. Where that distinction changes a claim, it is stated.

One instrument check was run before any measurement was trusted: the pane reported that nothing scrolls, but `document.documentElement.clientHeight` was 5,594px against a `scrollHeight` of 5,594px — the viewport was simply taller than the document. **That is environmental, not a product defect**, and no scroll finding appears below. See §16 for the four other candidates that were chased and killed.

## 1. Executive summary

LUMEE BOOTH Photobooth is a genuinely good product with no working way to pay for it, no working way to contact anyone about it, and no way for a free user to be told why the thing they just spent five steps configuring did nothing.

The craft is not in question. The rendering engine, the consent architecture and `product.js` are better than most funded products at this stage — `product.js` in particular is the best-designed file in the repository and should be the standard the rest of the codebase is held to. The landing page demonstrates the product using the *real* renderers rather than mocked screenshots, which is an unusually honest choice and it shows.

The commercial layer is the problem, and it is not one bug. Every single exit from the product is disconnected:

- All three paid tiers (£30 / £50 / £100) return **"This service is not available yet."**
- All four "TALK TO US" Business CTAs point at `https://mybishbash.app/contact`, which returns **404**.
- A free user who completes the five-step "Customise My Booth" flow and presses save gets **nothing at all** — no message, no upgrade prompt, no state written.

The site currently advertises four prices and sells none of them. That is the finding that matters, and it is why the single recommendation at the end is not "fix the checkout" but something narrower and more urgent.

Beneath that sit three structural issues worth naming before the rewrite: the product is invisible to the channel it is actually distributed through (zero Open Graph tags on a product whose growth loop is a link pasted into a party group chat), it is origin-bound in a way the `mybishbash.app` move will silently break, and it is being sold to UK consumers with no terms, no privacy policy and no refund policy anywhere on the site.

**If you read nothing else:** the product works and the shop is shut. Fix the shop before the rewrite, because the rewrite will not fix it and may bury it.

## 2. Root-cause map

Twenty-three findings, six causes. Fixing the six fixes the twenty-three.

### RC-1 — "The shopfront with no till"
*Every commercial exit is disconnected, and the site does not know it.*

The landing page renders prices, a founding-customer scarcity ribbon and four contact CTAs, all of which terminate in nothing. Because each failure is handled locally and quietly, the page looks healthy while converting zero.

Symptoms: F-01 (dead checkout ×3), F-02 (dead Business contact ×4), F-03 (silent Personal save), F-11 (founding counter never populates).

### RC-2 — "Silent refusal"
*The product's idiom for "you are not allowed to do that" is to do nothing and say nothing.*

`savePersonalSettings` returns `false`. `openPersonalSettings` is wired to landing-page links with no entitlement check in front of it. The camera path is the inverse failure — a single undifferentiated `alert()` for six distinct causes. In both directions the user is left without a next step, and in both directions the fix is the same: name the reason, offer the route.

Symptoms: F-03, F-04 (Safari-only camera alert), F-12 (no upsell at any point of denial).

### RC-3 — "Invisible to its own channel"
*This product spreads by someone pasting a link into a group chat, and the link is built to look like nothing.*

Zero `og:*` and zero `twitter:*` tags. No canonical. No JSON-LD. No `sitemap.xml`, no `robots.txt`. `/business` ships byte-identical `<title>` and `<meta description>` to `/`. A 2.23 MB PNG on the critical path. And the camera error tells everyone to use Safari — including the Android and in-app-browser users who make up much of a shared party link's audience.

Symptoms: F-05, F-06, F-07, F-08, F-04, F-17, F-18.

### RC-4 — "Origin-bound product on a moving domain"
*Every piece of user state is scoped to the origin the product is about to leave.*

Settings, gallery, edition counter and the verified-access token all live in `localStorage`/IndexedDB on `raes-photo-booth.vercel.app`. Moving to `mybishbash.app/photobooth` strands all of it. Separately, the subpath itself has three concrete breakages (trailing slash, `/business` routing, no catch-all).

Symptoms: F-09, F-10, F-19, F-20.

### RC-5 — "Compliance skipped rather than decided"
*Not a considered trade-off — the layer was simply never built.*

Selling £30–£100 products to UK consumers with no terms, no privacy policy, no refund/cancellation notice, and no route to any of them from any page. The Business surface additionally describes collecting email addresses and consent records. `/privacy` and `/terms` both 404.

Symptoms: F-13, F-14.

### RC-6 — "Client-side entitlement"
*What Personal buys is enforced only in JavaScript that ships to every visitor.*

`legacyProfileAvailable` — a grandfathering flag derived from a `localStorage` key any visitor can write — is OR'd into the Personal gate in three places. This is a symptom of the larger fact that entitlement is a browser variable.

Symptoms: F-15, F-16.

## 4. What is genuinely good

This list is not diplomacy. Several of these are better than the equivalent work in funded products, and the last one is the standard the rest of the repo should be held to.

**`product.js` is the quality benchmark — propagate its pattern.** 802 lines, zero dependencies, deep-frozen throughout, and it contains no checkout, storage or rendering code by explicit design. The comment at [product.js:92](../../product.js:92) — *"UI copy or a price change must never grant a feature"* — is enforced structurally: `PLAN_METADATA` and `CAPABILITY_MATRIX` are separate objects, so a price edit cannot widen access. `CHECKOUT_POLICY` ([product.js:154](../../product.js:154)) states in code that a success redirect grants nothing and only a verified webhook can. Most products discover that rule after a chargeback.

**The consent architecture is the real Business differentiator, and it is correctly built.** `validateConsentSubmission` ([product.js:376](../../product.js:376)) refuses to infer marketing permission from an email address, requires publicity consent as a separate explicit decision before any photo may be collected, and `buildConsentRecord` ([product.js:574](../../product.js:574)) refuses to write a record without the *exact wording and its version*. That last detail is what makes a consent record evidentially useful rather than decorative, and almost nobody does it.

**Grading is a pixel pass, with the reason written down.** `ctx.filter` shipped in Safari 17 and fails *silently* before it — so on an older booth iPad the filter buttons did nothing while the pixel-based cover finish kept working. The README documents the failure and the fix together. This is exactly the institutional memory that stops a future session "simplifying" it back.

**Only device-resident fonts are offered, and specimens are drawn on canvas.** `fonts.js` is the single place a typeface is named. The reasoning — *a web font is not a font, it is a request that might not arrive* — is correct for a booth on event Wi-Fi, and drawing specimens on canvas rather than in the DOM means the preview cannot promise something the covers won't keep.

**The service worker excludes entitlement data from shared Cache Storage.** [sw.js:65](../../sw.js:65) caches only a finite enumerated shell and explicitly refuses to cache anything carrying an `Authorization` header. That is a thought-through decision, not a default.

**Accessibility fundamentals that most sites miss are present.** A real Tab press produces a 3px `rgb(35,87,255)` outline that correctly matches `:focus-visible`. `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured in the stylesheet. `lang="en"` is set. Contrast passes AA on 7 of 8 sampled text roles.

**The landing page demonstrates the product with the product.** `marketing.js` drives the same `Covers`, `Polaroid` and strip renderers the booth uses, from three ordinary input photographs. Nothing on that page is a mockup. When the demo chips change the frame or filter, real pixels are re-rendered.

**Both test suites are green and meaningfully scoped.** 17/17 browser tests, 14/14 worker tests, covering consent independence, Stripe signature verification, payment lifecycle and brand-asset content-sniffing. The safety net the implementation programme will need already exists — a rarer starting position than it sounds.

## 7. Detailed findings

### F-01 — Every paid tier says the shop is shut
**Severity:** Blocker · **Category:** Backend / Product Strategy
**Affected users:** 100% of prospective paying customers
**Evidence:** Clicked `[data-checkout-plan="PERSONAL_6_MONTH"]` on the live site. `#checkoutStatus` renders **"This service is not available yet."** with class `checkout-status error`. `curl https://raes-photo-booth.vercel.app/v1/plans` → `404`. Identical for all three plans.
**Root cause:** `startCheckout` ([app.js:1534](../../app.js:1534)) posts to `/v1/billing/checkout`. `API_BASE` resolves to `""` because `<meta name="photobooth-api-base" content="">` is empty ([index.html:12](../../index.html:12)), so the request goes same-origin to Vercel, which serves static files only. The Worker in `worker/` is written, tested (14/14 passing) and **not deployed**.
**Recommended direction:** Either deploy the Worker and proxy `/v1`, or — if that is not imminent — replace the three price CTAs with an email-capture waitlist. Advertising a price you cannot take is worse than advertising no price.

### F-02 — The Business funnel has no contact route at all
**Severity:** Blocker · **Category:** Product Strategy
**Affected users:** 100% of Business prospects — the highest-value tier
**Evidence:** Four CTAs (`TALK TO US` ×3, `Talk to us` ×1) all resolve to `https://mybishbash.app/contact`. `curl -L` → **404**. Site-wide there are **zero** `mailto:` and **zero** `tel:` links; the only external link on either surface is that one dead URL.
**Root cause:** `<meta name="business-contact-url" content="https://mybishbash.app/contact">` ([index.html:11](../../index.html:11)) points at a page that has never existed. `mybishbash.app/` itself returns 200, so the domain is live and only this path is missing.
**Recommended direction:** Point at a `mailto:` today — it costs one line and cannot 404. Build the contact page before the Business surface gets any traffic.

### F-03 — Customise My Booth silently discards a free user's work
**Severity:** Blocker · **Category:** UX
**Affected users:** Every free user who engages with the Personal upsell — i.e. the most interested segment
**Evidence:** Reproduced live: clicked `#openPersonalSetup` from the landing page → settings screen opened with all five steps → typed `Sam's 30th` into `#setEventTitle` → clicked `#saveSettings`. Result: screen unchanged, **no visible message**, and `Object.keys(localStorage)` returned **`[]`** — nothing written.
**Root cause:** `savePersonalSettings` ([app.js:1494](../../app.js:1494)) opens with `if(!capabilities.canPersonaliseEvent&&!legacyProfileAvailable)return false;` — a bare early return with no user-facing consequence. Meanwhile `#openPersonalSetup` and `#openPersonalSetupSecondary` ([app.js:1690-1691](../../app.js:1690)) call `openPersonalSettings` with **no entitlement check at all**, so free users are actively invited into a flow whose exit is disabled.
**Recommended direction:** The gate belongs at the entrance or at the exit, not silently at the exit. Best: let free users configure everything, then convert the save button into the paywall — they have already done the work and seen their own event name in the preview, which is the strongest possible moment to ask. That turns the single worst bug into the best conversion point on the site.

### F-04 — One Safari-flavoured alert for six different failures
**Severity:** High · **Category:** UX / Copy
**Affected users:** Every user whose camera does not start — heavily concentrated in in-app browsers
**Evidence:** Live: clicking START PHOTOBOOTH with camera unavailable fires a native dialog reading **"Please allow camera access in Safari and try again."** The app then returns to the landing screen with no other indication.
**Root cause:** [app.js:931](../../app.js:931). A single `catch` wraps the whole capture routine and never inspects `err.name`, so `NotAllowedError` (denied), `NotFoundError` (no camera), `NotReadableError` (device busy), an insecure context, a `video.play()` rejection and a gallery-write failure all produce the same Safari-specific sentence.
**Why this is worse than it looks:** the product's distribution channel is a link pasted into a WhatsApp or Instagram group. Those links open in in-app browsers, several of which restrict `getUserMedia` outright, and none of which are Safari. The single most likely real-world failure mode is a guest at a party being told to fix Safari inside Instagram.
**Recommended direction:** Branch on `err.name`, replace the native `alert()` with an in-page state, and add an explicit "open in Safari/Chrome" route for in-app browsers. **[verify by hand]** — confirm actual behaviour by opening the live link from inside WhatsApp and Instagram on a real iPhone and Android handset; the audit browser cannot reproduce in-app browser restrictions.

### F-05 — A shareable product with nothing to share
**Severity:** High · **Category:** Frontend / Product Strategy
**Affected users:** Everyone who receives a link — the primary acquisition path
**Evidence:** Enumerated every `<meta>` on both surfaces. Present: `viewport`, `theme-color`, three `apple-mobile-web-app-*`, `description`, `business-contact-url`, `https://mybishbash-photobooth-api.summer-water-d053.workers.dev`. **`og:*` count: 0. `twitter:*` count: 0.** `<link rel="canonical">`: absent. JSON-LD blocks: 0.
**Root cause:** `<head>` is hand-authored ([index.html:3-17](../../index.html:3)) and social metadata was never added.
**Recommended direction:** Full `og:`/`twitter:` set with a rendered keepsake as `og:image`. The product literally generates beautiful shareable images; the link that sells it should carry one.

### F-06 — `/business` is metadata-identical to `/`
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** Frontend
**Evidence:** At `https://diy.lumeebooth.com/business`: `document.title` = `"LUMEE BOOTH Photobooth"`, description = *"Take three photos and turn them into a photo strip, magazine cover and moving Polaroid."* — byte-identical to the homepage, and no canonical on either.
**Root cause:** Both routes serve the same static `index.html` (`vercel.json` rewrite) and the client picks a surface at runtime; nothing updates the head.
**Recommended direction:** Set title/description/canonical/`og:` per surface on route selection.

### F-07 — No `robots.txt`, no `sitemap.xml`
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** Frontend
**Evidence:** Both return `404`, `text/plain`, 79 bytes.
**Recommended direction:** Add both at the final domain, not before — they should name `mybishbash.app/photobooth`.

### F-08 — Unbranded, unnavigable 404
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** UX
**Evidence:** `/nonexistent-page-xyz` → `404`, `content-type: text/plain`, 79 bytes. No branding, no link home.
**Root cause:** `vercel.json` declares three explicit rewrites and no catch-all, so unmatched paths fall through to the platform default.
**Recommended direction:** Branded 404 that routes to the booth. This matters more after the migration, where mistyped `/photobooth/...` paths become likely.

### F-09 — The domain move strands every existing user
**Severity:** High · **Category:** Architecture
**Affected users:** Every current organiser and returning guest
**Evidence:** All persistence is origin-scoped: `SETTINGS_KEY`, `ACCESS_KEY`, `GALLERY_DB`, `EDITION_KEY` ([app.js:95-100](../../app.js:95)) across `localStorage` and IndexedDB. Browsers scope both by origin, and `raes-photo-booth.vercel.app` → `mybishbash.app` is an origin change.
**Root cause:** The existing migration path (`raePhotoBoothLiveSettings` → new keys, [app.js:226-236](../../app.js:226)) is a *same-origin key rename*. It has no cross-origin equivalent, and cannot have one without an explicit export/import or a server round-trip.
**Mitigation that already exists:** paid access can be recovered via the email restore flow (`/v1/entitlements/restore/request`) — good design, and it means money is not lost even though device state is. Settings, gallery and edition counter have **no** recovery path.
**Recommended direction:** Ship an export/import of settings before the cutover, and keep the old origin serving a redirect plus a one-time "move your booth" hand-off. Decide explicitly whether the gallery is worth carrying (§17).

### F-10 — Legacy grandfathering will silently expire at the new origin
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** Architecture
**Evidence:** `legacyProfileAvailable` is derived from `localStorage.raePhotoBoothLiveSettings` ([app.js:161](../../app.js:161)), which will not exist on `mybishbash.app`.
**Root cause:** Same origin-scoping as F-09.
**Recommended direction:** A deliberate decision, not an accident: either carry grandfathering across in the hand-off, or end it and say so.

### F-11 — Founding scarcity is asserted, never evidenced
**Severity:** Low · **Category:** Copy
**Evidence:** `#foundingAvailability` reads *"Limited to the first 500 Founding Lifetime customers."* `loadFoundingAvailability()` calls `/v1/billing/founding`, which 404s, so the live remaining count never loads.
**Root cause:** Undeployed Worker (RC-1). The static fallback is a reasonable degradation.
**Recommended direction:** Once live, show the real remaining number — an unevidenced scarcity claim is weaker than none, and this one is verifiable by design (`remainingQuantitySource: "verified_purchase_records"`).

### F-12 — No upgrade route exists at any point of denial
**Severity:** High · **Category:** Product Strategy
**Evidence:** The only paths to pricing are `#choosePersonalPlan` and one post-checkout scroll ([app.js:1581](../../app.js:1581), [app.js:1689](../../app.js:1689)). Nothing in the settings screen, the booth or the free branding notice links to pricing.
**Root cause:** RC-2. Denial is implemented as absence rather than as an offer.
**Recommended direction:** Every locked capability should name its plan and link to it.

### F-13 — Selling to UK consumers with no terms, privacy policy or refund notice
**Severity:** Blocker · **Category:** Product Strategy / Compliance
**Evidence:** `/privacy` → 404. `/terms` → 404. Neither is linked from either footer. Meanwhile the site advertises £30/£50/£100 purchases and the Business surface describes collecting email addresses, marketing consent and publicity consent.
**Root cause:** RC-5 — the layer was never built.
**Why it is a Blocker and not paperwork:** UK Consumer Contracts Regulations require pre-contract information and cancellation rights for distance sales; a privacy policy is required once personal data is processed; and Stripe's own account review expects reachable terms and privacy URLs. This can block the very payment integration that F-01 is waiting on.
**Recommended direction:** Terms, privacy and a cancellation/refund statement, linked from both footers, before the first live transaction. Note the genuinely strong position to write from: photos never leave the device on Free and Personal.

### F-14 — Privacy claims are made in marketing but nowhere binding
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** Copy / Compliance
**Evidence:** The landing page states *"Photos stay on your device. There is no backend photo upload."* — true, and well-evidenced in code. But it appears only as marketing copy, with no policy document behind it.
**Recommended direction:** Promote it into the privacy policy, where it becomes a commitment rather than a claim. It is the product's best trust asset and currently the least durable.

### F-15 — A `localStorage` key unlocks Personal
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** Backend
**Evidence:** The Personal gate is `!capabilities.canPersonaliseEvent && !legacyProfileAvailable` at [app.js:435](../../app.js:435), [app.js:1495](../../app.js:1495) and [app.js:1503](../../app.js:1503). `legacyProfileAvailable` is set from `localStorage.getItem("raePhotoBoothLiveSettings")` ([app.js:161](../../app.js:161)) — a key any visitor can write from the console. Confirmed writable on the live origin; the flag is read at load, so a reload applies it.
**Root cause:** A legitimate grandfathering decision implemented with a client-writable signal.
**Severity rationale — deliberately not High:** for a £30 consumer product this is a low-value target, and it is not uniquely weak, because *all* Personal enforcement is client-side (F-16). It is listed because the intent (grandfather genuine legacy users) and the mechanism (trust any browser that claims to be one) have drifted apart.
**Recommended direction:** Grandfather by issuing real entitlements to known legacy users, or accept the bypass explicitly as a documented decision. What is not sustainable is an undocumented honour system.

### F-16 — Entitlement is a browser variable
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** Architecture
**Evidence:** `entitlement` is module state ([app.js:162](../../app.js:162)) hydrated from `localStorage[ACCESS_KEY]` and refreshed against `/v1/entitlements/current`. Branding policy is selected from it at render time ([app.js:407](../../app.js:407)).
**Root cause:** Inherent to a local-first static app — and largely the right trade. The honest framing is that the attribution baked into exports is a *courtesy*, not a control.
**Recommended direction:** Accept it and stop paying architectural cost for the pretence — or move export branding server-side for Business only, where white-labelling is actually worth money. Do not build DRM for a £30 party tool.

### F-17 — 2.23 MB of PNG is 96% of the page
**Severity:** High · **Category:** Frontend
**Affected users:** Every mobile visitor, disproportionately at venues on poor data
**Evidence:** Measured uncached transfer sizes: total critical path **2,331,527 bytes**, of which `assets/demo-photos.png` is **2,232,546 bytes**. Every other asset combined is 98,981 bytes compressed (`covers.js` 25.3 KB, `app.js` 23.6 KB, `styles.css` 10.8 KB, `index.html` 10.4 KB). Re-encoding the same 1536×1024 image at JPEG q82 yields **335,630 bytes — an 85% reduction**. It is also pre-cached by the service worker ([sw.js:3](../../sw.js:3)), so every install pays it.
**Additional waste:** `marketing.js` crops to `SOURCE_CROP_HEIGHT = 640` from 1024 rows ([marketing.js:10](../../marketing.js:10)) — **37% of the downloaded pixels are never displayed**.
**Recommended direction:** Crop to the 640 rows actually used, encode as WebP with a JPEG fallback. Expect ~2.23 MB → under 200 KB with no visible change.

### F-18 — Three `<h1>` elements in one document
**Severity:** Low · **Category:** Frontend
**Evidence:** `"Your party.Your photobooth."`, `"Want your brand on the booth?"`, `"Your Celebration"` all present in the DOM; only one visible at a time.
**Root cause:** All surfaces are `.screen` sections in a single document.
**Recommended direction:** Low priority; resolve alongside per-surface metadata (F-06).

### F-19 — `/photobooth` without a trailing slash breaks every asset
**Severity:** High · **Category:** Architecture
**Affected users:** Everyone, from the moment the migration goes live
**Evidence:** Every reference is relative: `href="styles.css"`, `src="app.js"`, `href="manifest.webmanifest"` ([index.html:13-16](../../index.html:13)), `register("./sw.js")` ([app.js:1755](../../app.js:1755)), `"start_url": "./"`, and all 14 service-worker `ASSETS` entries. At `https://mybishbash.app/photobooth` the document base is `/`, so `styles.css` resolves to `/styles.css` — wrong. At `/photobooth/` it resolves to `/photobooth/styles.css` — correct.
**Root cause:** Relative paths are the right choice for a portable subpath app, but they make the trailing slash load-bearing.
**Recommended direction:** A permanent redirect from `/photobooth` to `/photobooth/` at the parent domain, plus a `<base>` tag as a belt-and-braces guard. Verify before cutover, because the failure mode is a totally unstyled page.

### F-20 — `/business` needs a rewrite the parent domain does not yet have
**Severity:** Medium · **Category:** Architecture
**Evidence:** `vercel.json` rewrites exactly `/`, `/business`, `/business/`. Under the subpath, the relative link `href="business"` ([index.html:30](../../index.html:30)) resolves to `/photobooth/business`, which the parent must rewrite to the same document. There is no catch-all, so any other `/photobooth/*` path yields the bare 404 of F-08.
**Recommended direction:** Rewrite `/photobooth/(.*)` to the deployment and let the client route, rather than enumerating paths in two places.
**Note:** one hard navigation to `/business` timed out during the audit while client-side routing to the same URL worked, and `curl` returned 200. Recorded as instrument noise, not a finding — but worth one manual check after migration. **[verify by hand]**

### F-21 — Mobile nav detaches from the header and lands on the hero
**Severity:** High · **Category:** UI
**Affected users:** Every visitor at ≤768px — the majority
**Evidence:** At 375×812: `.site-nav` occupies y=0–72. `.audience-nav` renders at **y=143**, 71px below the header it belongs to, in a bordered box overlapping `.hero-kicker` (y=166–196). Rect intersection confirmed programmatically. Visible in the mobile screenshot as a floating box over "FOR PERSONAL EVENTS".
**Root cause:** [styles.css:364](../../styles.css:364) sets `.audience-nav{grid-row:2;grid-column:1/-1;position:absolute;top:72px}`. For an absolutely-positioned **grid item**, the containing block is its **grid area**, not the grid container's padding box — so `top:72px` is measured from the top of implicit row 2 (~y=71), not from the header's top edge, and the 72px header height is counted twice.
**Recommended direction:** Drop `grid-row`/`grid-column` from the absolutely-positioned element, or use `top:100%`. The intended design (a tab bar hanging off the header's bottom border) is clear and correct — only the containing block is wrong.

### F-22 — Personal sells six months of a one-evening event
**Severity:** High · **Category:** Product Strategy
**Evidence:** `PLAN_METADATA` ([product.js:107-138](../../product.js:107)): £30/6 months, £50/12 months, £100 lifetime (capped at 500).
**The mismatch:** the customer has *one party*. Nobody has six months of birthdays. Pricing in months forces buyers to convert a duration into an event they have already scheduled, and invites the question "will I still want this in month five?" — which has no good answer. The £30 tier is the weakest: at £5/month against £4.17/month for the annual, it is dominated on both price and duration, and exists mainly to anchor £50.
**The strongest counter-argument, considered:** durations are trivial to implement, map cleanly onto Stripe, and Founding Lifetime genuinely targets a different buyer (the frequent host and the early supporter), where £100 is well judged. That is real — but it argues for keeping *lifetime*, not for pricing the entry tier in months.
**Recommended direction:** Sell **the event**. "One event, £X" matches how the purchase is actually reasoned about, removes the expiry objection, and makes the upsell to Founding Lifetime a straightforward "host more than twice and this pays for itself." Keep Founding Lifetime unchanged. **Cost of being wrong:** low and reversible — pricing is presentational metadata in a file explicitly designed so prices grant nothing, so this is among the cheapest changes in the whole audit.

### F-23 — The paywall undersells itself
**Severity:** High · **Category:** Copy / Product Strategy
**Evidence:** The Free pricing card reads *"Use the full photobooth with MyBishBash-branded outputs."* But `launchFreeBooth` ([app.js:1501](../../app.js:1501)) resets free users to `DEFAULTS`, where `eventTitle` is **`"Your Celebration"`** ([app.js:2](../../app.js:2)). Because cover copy generates from the event title, **every free magazine cover is mastheaded "YOUR CELEBRATION"** rather than the guest's actual party.
**Why this matters commercially:** the real free/paid difference is not a discreet credit line — it is whether the keepsake says *Rae's 26th Birthday* or *Your Celebration*. That is a far stronger reason to pay, it is already built, and the pricing page hides it. Meanwhile the copy leads with removing branding, which is the weaker lever.
**Recommended direction:** Show it, don't describe it. Put the two covers side by side on the pricing card. The comparison the landing page already builds for the welcome screen ("FREE / READY TO GO" vs "PERSONALISED / YOUR EVENT") is exactly right — it is simply applied to the least valuable difference.

## 8. Accessibility findings

Measured, not assumed. Mostly good, with two real defects.

**Genuinely good:** a real Tab press produces `outline: 3px solid rgb(35,87,255)` and correctly matches `:focus-visible` — verified by keypress, not by `.focus()`. `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured. `lang="en"` present. Contrast passes AA on 7 of 8 sampled roles, several comfortably (hero subhead 18.88:1).

**F-24 — Kicker labels fail AA.** *Severity: Medium.* `.product-kicker` ("FOR PERSONAL EVENTS", section numbers) computes `rgb(217,45,127)` bold 10px on its pink pill at **3.60:1**, below the 4.5:1 required for text under 18.66px bold. Darken the pink or lighten the pill.

**F-25 — 22 sub-44px touch targets.** *Severity: Medium.* On the mobile landing surface, 22 controls are under 44×44 CSS px, including all nine demo frame/filter chips at **29px tall** (`White` 42×29, `Film` 35×29, `B&W` 39×29). **Precision matters here:** at 29px these *pass* WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (24×24 minimum) — this is not an AA failure. It is a usability finding against Apple's 44pt guidance, and it lands on the interactive demo that does the selling on mobile. Raise the chips to 44px.

**F-26 — No skip link.** *Severity: Low.* No skip-to-content link; keyboard users traverse the full nav on every surface change.

**Not assessed:** the booth capture flow's keyboard path and screen-reader behaviour, since the camera could not be started. **[verify by hand]**

## 13. Decisions required

These are yours; the spec cannot be written without them.

1. **Does the Worker ship before or after the migration?** If after, the prices must come off the page in the meantime (F-01). Leaving them is the worst option.
2. **Is the gallery carried across origins?** Export/import is real work and photos are large. Settings alone is much cheaper and covers most of the pain. Recommendation: settings yes, gallery no, with a clear warning on the old origin.
3. **Do legacy free users keep Personal on the new domain?** (F-10.) Either is defensible; drifting into "no" by accident is not.
4. **Event pricing or duration pricing?** (F-22.) Recommendation: event.
5. **Does Business get a public price?** Currently "contact us" with no anchor and a dead link. A "from £X" would qualify leads before they reach a form you have to build.

## 14. The one immediate recommendation

**Reconnect the three exits before anything else — and do it this week, in this order: the contact `mailto:`, the checkout decision, the silent save.**

Not the migration. Not the rewrite. Not the engine.

The reasoning: the migration changes the product's address, and none of these three failures is caused by its address. Moving first means carrying every one of them onto the new domain and re-testing them there, while `mybishbash.app/photobooth` earns its first impressions on a site that cannot take money or answer an email. The rewrite also puts a large, plausible-looking piece of work in front of three fixes that between them are perhaps a day.

**The strongest alternative considered:** migrate first, on the argument that F-19 and F-20 are structural, the domain is the branding decision everything else hangs off, and fixing commerce twice is wasteful. That argument is real — but it inverts the cost. The commercial fixes are near-zero-risk and independent of the domain; the migration is the risky, irreversible-feeling step. Doing the cheap independent work first costs almost nothing and means the new domain launches with a working shop.

**What it costs to be wrong:** very little. If the migration turns out to be more urgent — a booked event, a brand deadline — these three fixes travel with the code and are not invalidated by the move. The reverse is not true.
